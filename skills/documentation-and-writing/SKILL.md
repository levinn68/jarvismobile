# Documentation & Professional Writing

## Before drafting
- Identify audience, purpose, desired action, tone, and required format.
- Preserve factual details, product names, paths, and constraints.
- Determine whether the artifact is a specification, guide, runbook, report, or message.

## Quality
- Lead with the information the reader needs.
- Use precise headings and consistent terminology.
- Prefer active voice and concrete instructions.
- Separate prerequisites, steps, expected results, and troubleshooting.
- Include examples only when accurate and useful.
- Mark assumptions and version-specific details.
- Avoid filler, unsupported claims, and unnecessary jargon.

## Technical docs
Include purpose, prerequisites, configuration, commands, expected output, failure modes, security notes, and rollback where relevant.
